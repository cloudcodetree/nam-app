Interaction study · six modelers

How the others actually work

Not what they look like — what happens when you touch them. Drawn from manuals, firmware notes and owner complaints across HeadRush Prime, Quad Cortex, Nano Cortex, Helix Stadium, Darkglass Anagram and Dimehead.

01 · The convergenceEvery vendor built the same three tiers, for the same reason

This is the most important structural fact in the whole study, and it isn’t an aesthetic convention — it’s a workaround for a physical cost.

TierWhat it isCost
Preset / RigThe whole chain. Loading re-initialises every block.HAS A GAP
Scene / SnapshotParameter + bypass states within the loaded chain.INSTANT
StompOne block’s bypass, live under the foot.INSTANT

Changing presets causes “an expected split second of audio dropout, as switching between Presets re-initializes the whole signal chain.”

Neural DSP support — and this single sentence is why the scene tier exists at all

The middle tier was invented because the top tier is expensive. Scenes are gapless precisely because everything is already loaded. That is the entire trick, and it is available to us for free.

02 · The ruleWhat a scene is not allowed to change

Every vendor independently forbids the scene layer from touching anything that would force reallocation. They are explicit about it:

“All snapshots in a particular preset share the same models; you cannot, for example, add a US Deluxe Amp block in Snapshot 1 and switch its model to a Brit Plexi in Snapshot 2.”

Line 6 Helix manual

Nobody arrived at this by taste. They arrived at it because a scene that can swap a model is just a preset with extra steps — it reintroduces the gap, and then the tier has no reason to exist.

What this settles for us

A scene changes engine parameters and bypass states. It never touches the model or the IR. Different amp means different rig. That one rule makes every scene recall instant, allocation-free and incapable of failing — no load state, no crossfade problem, no failure path to design.

03 · Switch budgetsBelow six switches, everyone builds a mode system

Switch counts in the wild: 3 (HX Stomp, Anagram), 4 (Dimehead, Nano Cortex ×2, Chocolate Plus), 6 (Fractal FC-6, Morningstar MC6), 8–12 (Helix, Quad Cortex, HeadRush Prime).

UnitHow it stretches its switches
Quad CortexFour global modes — Preset / Scene / Stomp / Hybrid. Hybrid assigns a different mode to each row, so you don’t have to pick one.
HeadRush PrimeFive modes across 12 switches; four corners spent on navigation. Mode changes by holding the top-right switch.
Helix StadiumCapacitive touch as a third state: rest a toe to jump the screen to that switch’s assignment without pressing it.
AnagramThree switches, chords for banking. A+B previews the previous bank, B+C the next.
Nano CortexTwo switches that are also rotary encoders — press, hold, and twist.

The trap this sets for us

The industry answer to a small switch count is a mode system, and every mode system is entered by a hold or a chord.

We can do neither. In HID keyboard mode the pedal sends no key-up, so hold is undetectable. And a two-switch chord may be swallowed by the pedal’s own firmware as a bank change and never reach the app at all.

So we have four switches, no modes, and no gesture to change modes with — the scarcest budget in the study, with the fewest tools. Anything built on holds or chords is theatre.

Which forces the answer, and the answer is good: put all four switches on scenes. Four instant, gapless, cannot-fail actions. Rigs change by thumb between songs, where a gap is free. This is the one layout that spends a scarce budget on the tier that costs nothing.

04 · Chain editingReordering is optional — two competitors ship without it

We have been treating a reorderable chain as table stakes. The two products closest to ours don’t have one:

  • Nano Cortex — “the chain is FIXED and cannot be reordered anywhere, not on the device, not in the app.” Seven fixed slots. Quad Cortex presets can’t even be loaded, explicitly because of it.
  • Dimehead — a NAM player with no chain editing at all. Editing means “select what goes in each fixed slot and turn its knobs.” 128 flat presets, no scenes, no modes, no layered abstraction. A deliberate simplicity choice.
  • Free drag-reorder on a phone is the minority position, and where it exists it’s paired with an unbounded chain — Mooer GE Labs offers unlimited drag-and-drop effects, and on an iPhone SE the amp labels are obscured by the effects below them.

Our ↑↓ buttons already sidestep the single most destructive defect in the corpus: on the Anagram, drag-to-reorder and swipe-to-scroll are the same gesture and the drag wins — “successful scrolling is more of a lottery.”

05 · The closest analogueHeadRush already shipped our exact constraint

As of HeadRush OS v5.1, exactly one NAM file can be loaded natively per rig.

HeadRush Prime / Core / Flex Prime firmware notes, August 2026

A $999 flagship with a 7-inch touchscreen landed on the same one-model-per-rig limit we have. It is not a deficiency to design around — it is the shape of the category. And their answer to it is instructive: the rig owns everything else — footswitch assignments, scene definitions, expression assignments, a colour, a tempo, a MIDI program number. The model is one ingredient; the rig is the performance unit.

Where we differ, and it matters

In our app a rig is {uid, name, chain, extra}. It owns no engine parameters at all — gain and EQ are global and follow you across rig changes, and two rigs cannot be level-matched.

Every product in this study makes the preset own its parameters. That is the gap to close first, because scenes are meaningless until a rig owns something for a scene to override.

06 · DecisionsWhat this study actually settles

  • Scenes flip engine nodes, never the model or IR. Industry-wide rule, arrived at independently by every vendor.
  • All four switches go to scenes. We can’t build a mode system, so we shouldn’t need one.
  • A rig must own its engine parameters. Currently it owns none. This blocks scenes, level-matching, and the claim that a rig is a sound.
  • Keep the ↑↓ reorder. Two close competitors ship no reordering at all; drag on a scroll surface is the corpus’s worst defect.
  • Nothing hides behind a hold or a chord. Not a preference — the hardware cannot express either.
