# Guitar-modeler UX research (2026-08-22)

Nine parallel research agents over HeadRush Prime/Core/Flex Prime, Neural DSP
Quad Cortex + Nano Cortex, Line 6 Helix Stadium/XL, Darkglass Anagram,
Dimehead, plus three cross-cutting lenses (stage-gear heuristics, mobile
chain editing, MIDI/expression binding UX). Synthesized, then attacked.

**Read the critique before the doctrine.** The synthesis's load-bearing claim
-- that one model + one IR leaves 'no cheap layer to hide behind' -- is FALSE,
and two principles collapse on it. Verified against Source/dsp/ToneEngine.h.

## Antipattern corpus (the durable part)

Real, sourced failure modes from shipping products. Use as a regression
checklist for any performance screen.

- Darkglass Anagram — swipe-to-scroll and drag-to-reorder are the same gesture and the drag wins: 'you will never miss dragging a block, but successful scrolling is more of a lottery', and 'in the preset manager: each time I try to scroll through the list of presets, I'm moving a preset to another location inadvertently'. No undo, no confirmation; recovery is switching preset away and back. Acknowledged by Darkglass on Facebook 4 Feb 2026 ('It's in our backlog'), still unfixed at v1.17.1 six months later.
- Darkglass Anagram — footswitches fire on RELEASE (because long-press is overloaded onto the same three switches), and the official advice is that musicians must learn to 'Press+Release the footswitch slightly ahead (i.e. earlier) than what you might be used to… If this is something that you find you can't get used to, then a MIDI footswitch might be a good alternative.' Only the looper was ever changed to fire on press.
- Darkglass Anagram — the A+B / B+C bank-change chords and the long-press overlays cannot be disabled: 'the footswitches are close together if you have large/wide shoes' produces accidental bank changes, and holding a beat too long 'when trying to time the release of the footswitch to change Scenes at a precise moment of a song' dumps you into the Tuner or Looper mid-song.
- Darkglass Anagram — endless encoders with speed-dependent acceleration and no numeric entry: 'It's absolutely insane that it takes a solid five minutes of turning and praying to hit exactly 70.0Hz on the dang HPF.' They disabled touch on continuous parameters in v1.14 as a regression fix, and only added a numeric keypad in v1.16, a year after launch.
- Darkglass Anagram — expression endpoints shipped broken three separate times (v1.14.0 'parameters in Expression Binding Macros not reaching min and max', v1.16.3 'expression binding parameters not reaching exact endpoints'), plus a documented crash when driving Parametric EQ frequency from the pedal.
- HeadRush Prime/Core/Flex — press-vs-release lag from overloading hold onto the action switch, admitted in their own FAQ. The fix was a setting, not a redesign: v4.0's global 'On Press' option 'disables hold functionality', so enabling it costs you the entire shortcut layer and forces you to spend real footswitches on View, Hands-Free, Looper and Tuner.
- HeadRush Prime — a scene exists only as a MODE of a footswitch, so there is no scene list, no scene count, and no way to see all of a rig's scenes at once; you discover them by walking the Hardware Assign grid one box at a time.
- Neural DSP Quad Cortex — parameters are preset-global until you long-press one for ~2 seconds and an 'ABCD' icon appears. It is the top new-user trap, and commercially downloaded presets routinely ship with no scene assignments at all, so buyers hit it on day one.
- Neural DSP Quad Cortex — swipe-up-for-Gig-View had 'no visual indication'; gigging players reported 'I have to kneel down and switch to Gig View mode by swiping', sometimes needing multiple swipes, and pressing bank up/down ejected them back to the Grid. Footswitch access to Gig View shipped in CorOS 4.0.0 (Jan 2026), five years after launch; boot tooltips landed in the same release.
- Neural DSP Quad Cortex — Scene-mode LEDs turn inactive switches fully OFF rather than dim, so there is nothing to aim at: 'it's really hard to hit the correct footswitch on a dark stage… the foot switches are so close together, it's way too easy to step on the wrong switch.'
- Neural DSP Quad Cortex — no global 'what does EXP1 control?' page; assignments are discovered by opening blocks one at a time, and ranges are set in 0-100% while the targets read in dB/ms/Hz, so a user with an EQ output at -5 dB could not work out what to enter as MAX RANGE.
- Neural DSP Quad Cortex — at least three invisible ways to silence a signal path (a splitter's mixer level, an output block's Mute/Solo, a per-block bypass behaviour set to mute), several of them scene-assignable, with nothing on the Grid indicating which one is active.
- Neural DSP Nano Cortex / Cortex Cloud — the app would not launch at all without an internet connection. An App Store reviewer at a festival with no signal 'couldn't change any of my sounds', calling it 'inexcusable and absolutely insane for $700-$2k hardware'; the app sits at 3.4/5, and the eventual Offline Mode (v2.8.0) covers only device settings.
- Neural DSP Nano Cortex — hold SAVE for 3s and it overwrites the preset you are currently playing; the documented workaround is 'go to the app and save into an empty slot instead'.
- Neural DSP Nano Cortex — the BANK button cycles Capture banks but does NOT change which presets the four footswitches recall, so you cannot move to a different set of four with your foot. The motivating feature request says it plainly: 'I don't want to pull out my phone to load presets into banks.'
- Neural DSP Nano Cortex — gapless preset switching was a launch selling point; NanOS 2.0.0 introduced a ~60ms gap (community-measured near 72ms) with no committed fix and no downgrade path. Owners: 'It is definitely not 0.06 seconds. The gap is very noticeable', 'a deal breaker for live shows', 'the pedal worthless'.
- Neural DSP Cortex Cloud — the BLE link drops when the user simply switches to a browser, and 'the app never reconnects unless you unplug the device'.
- Neural DSP Cortex Mobile — 'Totally inaccessible for screen readers… Specifically VoiceOver', a 1-star review with no vendor answer.
- Line 6 Helix — Command Center commands silently stack on top of an existing stomp assignment on the same switch, producing switches that 'needed a double press to work'; the diagnosis and fix both require opening HX Edit on a desktop.
- Line 6 Helix — momentary vs latching is scoped per FOOTSWITCH, not per assignment, so all of the up-to-8 blocks bound to that switch inherit one behaviour; and the 64-controller-assignments-per-preset ceiling surfaces only as 'Too many controller assignments!' with no running count anywhere.
- Line 6 Helix — two authorities over one piece of state: when a stomp switch and a snapshot both control the same block, the switch's apparent polarity silently inverts. The manual's own example is FS2 toggling Delay(ON)/Reverb(OFF) when a snapshot turns the Reverb on.
- Line 6 Helix Stadium — ~60 second boot time versus 15-20s on the original Helix. 'That's an eternity when you're standing in front of an audience.' Also: a full system backup requires powering into Maintenance Mode (a 57-second restart) and duplicating one means physically removing the microSD card.
- Dimehead NAM player — long-pressing into the tuner reloads the preset and silently discards unsaved edits: 'my gain reverts back to 2.0 which was there before. That is unfortunately a bug.' A destructive side effect on a performance gesture.
- Dimehead NAM player — no manual exists in any language; the MIDI CC map lives only on a browser page that can write to the device but explicitly 'cannot be read from the NAM player through this interface'. In their own tutorial the presenter computes '80% of 128 ≈ 102' on camera because no numeric value is displayed anywhere.
- Dimehead NAM player — the FAQ ships a dBu→input-gain lookup table (2 dBu→4.5, 8 dBu→3.3, 12 dBu→2.5) so users can hand-calibrate headroom; a factory-default setting made the reviewer's own capture sound 'absolutely broken' until gain dropped from 5.0 to 1.9.
- Dimehead NAM player — its own official Episode 6 tutorial abandons the unit's four footswitches for an external M-Vave Chocolate because they are 'spaced out a little bit wider, making it a little bit easier to not accidentally hit one of those footswitches'. The manufacturer conceding on its own channel that a €30 controller beats its €595 pedal's foot interface.
- Mooer GE Labs — 'unlimited effects in your chain' with drag-and-drop, and on an iPhone SE 'You cannot see the amp controls labels they are obscured by the effects below. Impossible to know what you're adjusting.'
- Positive Grid Spark app — double-tap is the way to swap amps and effects, and per Engadget 'there's no indication in the UI that double tapping is how you change out amps and effects'.
- Positive Grid BIAS FX 2 Mobile — the linear chain runs off the right edge with no zoom-out (users cite the 2009 POD X3 Live as prior art), and there is no stomp layer, so 'to do that right now requires using scene mode but each scene is effectively a new patch. I just want to add/take away delay/boost etc. one button at a time.'
- Morningstar MC6 editor — copy/paste scope is hidden behind click count: single click pastes to the editor, double click saves to the controller, triple click applies across all banks and is irreversible. The green Disconnect button also sits next to Save Preset and gets mis-clicked; an empty preset still renders all 32 message slots.
- FabFilter — documented silent overwrite: 'There is no warning when you associate a different knob with a controller number that is already used. It will just be replaced.' Ableton has the mirror failure: a parameter can only be driven by one control, so a second mapping silently deletes the first.
- Darkglass KosmOS v1.11.1 — had to ship a fix for 'audio backend crash when scrolling through cabinet files or neural models'. Browsing was wired straight into the audio graph.
- HeadRush firmware changelogs — the largest cluster of shipped bugs is state desync between surfaces: wrong LED colour when switching between rigs whose scenes had dual states, parameter knobs not updating when a rig held two copies of the same effect, and the web Remote Editor and the device disagreeing about a dismissed Save dialog.

## Synthesized principles (UNCORRECTED -- see critique)

### 1. Price every foot action in milliseconds before you bind it

**Rule:** Classify every bindable action as INSTANT (parameter/bypass inside the loaded model) or LOADING (swaps a .nam or an IR), label which it is in the binding sheet, and never let a LOADING action fire without either a crossfade or a visible load state. Test: open the binding sheet — if an action's row doesn't say which class it is, it fails.

**Why:** Every vendor's entire recommended workflow was reshaped by this one number, and none of them publish it. This app's constraint makes it worse, not better: with ONE model + ONE IR and no audible pedal nodes, EVERY audible change is a model swap, i.e. the expensive class. There is currently no cheap layer to hide behind.

*Evidence:* Neural DSP staff, verbatim: a preset change 're-initializes the whole signal chain' → 'an expected split second of audio dropout'. Darkglass Anagram cross-bank changes measured by owners at '1-2 seconds… unusable live for mid-song changes'. Nano Cortex shipped ~60ms (community ~72ms) in NanOS 2.0 after marketing gapless and got 'deal breaker for live shows' / 'the pedal worthless', no committed fix. Line 6 Stadium is single-DSP and the community answer is 'stop switching presets, use snapshots'. Dimehead solved it with a NAM Preloader (v1.0.4) and gigging owners cite it unprompted. Mechanism if you build it: US 11,423,870 B2 (two chains live briefly, amplitude ramp, tail buffer) — which is the repo's existing publish-then-retire shared_ptr hand-off extended by one chain.

### 2. A scene layer with nothing audible under it is a lie

**Rule:** Do not ship scenes/snapshots until at least one bypassable node is audible on the audio thread. Test: point at every control on the perform surface and name the audio-thread parameter it moves; if you can't, delete the control.

**Why:** Eight of nine reports say 'scenes are the performance unit' — but every one of those products has an effects chain whose bypass states a scene flips. This engine has none: pedal/post items are stored + visual only. A scene here would flip nothing you can hear, and would train the user to trust a control that does nothing.

*Evidence:* Repo decision 2026-08-15 (Chris): scenes, routing, footswitch assignment and PERFORM deleted, not hidden, because 'the surface had outgrown what the engine can actually do (ONE model + ONE IR, no multi-node DSP chain)'. Corroborated by the two products that never shipped scenes and review fine anyway: Nano Cortex (no scenes, no snapshots, no stomp mode) and Dimehead NAM player (flat 128 presets — the complaints are about chain depth and encoders, never about missing scenes). Counter-position on file: HeadRush, Fractal, Line 6 and QC all treat scenes as the primary live unit — but only because bypass is audible for them.

### 3. Fire on the first event; every hold and every chord taxes the switch it shares

**Rule:** One action per switch, fired on press/arrival. If a switch also carries a hold or participates in a chord, that switch's plain press is no longer instant — say so on its tile. Ship no double-tap at all in v1. Test: stomp A; the action must fire before your foot leaves the switch.

**Why:** Latency on the primary stomp is invisible in the lab and fatal on a downbeat, and it is always paid by the simple action, not the fancy one.

*Evidence:* HeadRush's own FAQ admits a dual-purpose switch 'will not turn on/off until the associated pedal is released'; they shipped a global 'On Press' option in v4.0 for 'a snappier feel' — which then disabled the entire hold shortcut layer and forced users to spend real switches on View/Hands-Free/Looper/Tuner. Darkglass Anagram fires on release and tells users to 'press+release slightly ahead… than what you might be used to'; a standing faction wants a global option and only the looper got fixed. Morningstar states it outright: assigning a Double Tap 'will add some latency' to that switch's plain Press 'whether or not your Switch Sensitivity setting is set to 5'. Nano Cortex recalls on release, with an open request against it. Fractal's best-regarded community layout (OMG9) deliberately triggers on the 'downstroke' for musical timing.

### 4. Bind what the pedal actually sends — never a CC table, never the vendor app

**Rule:** Primary assignment path is: touch the target on screen → 'Listening — stomp the switch you want' → capture whatever arrives. A CC/keycode table may exist only as an advanced escape hatch. Test: can a user bind a switch without knowing what a CC number is?

**Why:** The pedal's factory map is not knowable in advance, and on this specific unit it isn't even MIDI out of the box.

*Evidence:* Repo: Chocolate Plus 'factory defaults are contested — three sources, three answers', every value user-editable in CubeSuite, and OBSERVED on Chris's unit it ships in HID KEYBOARD mode (`dumpsys midi` reports zero devices until an app calls openBluetoothDevice()). Morningstar has no MIDI learn at all — you read CC numbers out of another manual and type them — and its own forum carries 'a bit surprised about the rather steep learning curve… the editor is often overly complex to use'. Helix's press-and-hold-the-knob → 'Learn Controller' is described by helixhelp as doable in ~2 seconds. A vguitarforums user on M-Vave's own CubeSuite: 'It's been one of the more frustrating things I've done.'

### 5. Show the raw event and let the user name its meaning, per binding

**Rule:** Every binding row displays the captured event verbatim ('CC20 ch·any: 127 on press, 0 on release' / 'Key: PageDown') next to a three-way FirePolicy control (Trigger on press / Toggle / Momentary), and a live monitor is visible while learning. Test: read a binding row aloud — it should describe both what arrives and how it's interpreted.

**Why:** Momentary vs toggle is undecidable from timing alone, so any heuristic will be wrong some of the time; the cure is that the user can see and override the guess without leaving the screen.

*Evidence:* Repo decisions.md: 'FirePolicy::Auto double-fires a momentary switch HELD longer than the 400ms release window… the Controllers UI MUST expose the policy per binding — that is now a requirement, not a nicety.' Loopy Pro's learn 'expects the controller to send an on message on press and an off message on release'; controllers that don't cause it to learn HOLD instead of ON. Helix scopes Momentary/Latching per FOOTSWITCH, so all up-to-8 blocks on that switch inherit one behaviour — a documented surprise. Anagram ships a 'Toggle Logic' setting (Value 0-63 off / 64-127 on, vs Toggle: any non-zero flips) precisely to rescue cheap controllers. Morningstar's Activity Monitor and QC's per-message test-send exist for the same diagnostic reason.

### 6. The perform screen is a different screen with different geometry, sized in arcminutes

**Rule:** Rig name ≥ ~64dp at 5 ft (ANSI/HFES 16-22 arcmin ⇒ 4.3-5.9mm cap at 3ft, 7.1-9.8mm at 5ft); ≤6 objects on screen; no thin strokes; no low-contrast greys. Test: screenshot at 360×640dp, print/view it from five feet in a dark room and read the rig name and which switch is live.

**Why:** Every product that got this right spent hardware on it, and the ones that scaled it down are the cautionary tales — a 6-inch phone at boot height is a harsher case than a 4-inch panel at floor height.

*Evidence:* Legibility arithmetic from ANSI/HFES via conceptron.com (no music-industry spec exists). guitar.com on the 4-inch HeadRush Flex Prime: 'horribly cramped'… 'the bass and treble controls aren't even on the same screen, because they simply won't fit'. Dimehead: 'even wearing glasses it's pretty hard to read the name of the NAM profiles' — the fix was Stage Mode's giant type. Anagram's Name View (giant preset name, knobs deliberately inert) is the single most-praised screen in that product, per both Premier Guitar and Guitar World. Mooer GE Labs on an iPhone SE: labels 'obscured by the effects below. Impossible to know what you're adjusting.'

### 7. Encode state by position first, brightness second, hue third — and never hue alone

**Rule:** Which switch is live must be readable from WHICH of the tiles is lit, with the screen in greyscale. Bright = engaged, dim-but-present = bypassed; never turn an inactive tile fully off. Every colour is paired with a text label. Test: render the perform screen greyscale and identify the live rig.

**Why:** Hue is the first thing lost to a floor-mounted phone's oblique viewing angle, to stage lighting, and to 1-in-12 men.

*Evidence:* Quad Cortex Scene mode leaves inactive switches fully OFF and performers report 'it's really hard to hit the correct footswitch on a dark stage'; Stomp mode's bright/dim is the mode to copy. Helix ring LEDs carry engaged/bypassed in BRIGHTNESS and category in hue. Nano Cortex is colour-only with no text and reviewers admit forgetting whether orange or blue meant pre or post, 'requiring phone app consultation to verify current settings' (guitar.com). WCAG 1.4.1; NN/g's greyscale test. Aviation's dark-cockpit rule — 'Light out philosophy' — for status-by-exception.

### 8. The phone IS the scribble strip — render the pedal's physical geometry, permanently

**Rule:** A persistent 6-slot map on the perform screen laid out in the controller's physical order (A B C D in a row; E drawn spanning A|B and F spanning C|D so the chord geometry teaches itself), each slot showing label + colour + lit/dim, tappable to fire and tappable-when-empty to assign. Test: cover the pedal — the screen alone must tell you what each switch does right now.

**Why:** The Chocolate Plus has a three-digit display and nothing else. Every hardware product without scribble strips solved this by drawing the switches on its screen, and the ones that didn't produced the single loudest complaint in the corpus.

*Evidence:* HeadRush Core and Flex Prime draw virtual OLED footswitch cards along the bottom of the touchscreen and you can TAP a card to fire the switch. Line 6 Helix Stadium (non-XL, no scribble strips) renders the 12 labels in Home > Play. QC's Gig View exists as Development Update #5 explicitly to 'visualize what the footswitches are assigned to instantly'. Against: Nano Cortex has no screen, and Premier Guitar's verdict is that without the phone 'you'll need to have a photographic memory of what presets are made up of what amps and effects'. Satisfies CLAUDE.md's rule that the bottom chrome is the only persistent chrome.

### 9. Switch order is not chain order, and a chord is not a switch

**Rule:** Model bindings as an explicit per-rig switch→action table with drag-one-card-onto-another to SWAP. Never put a mid-song-critical action on E(A+B) or F(C+D), and make combo actions QUEUE rather than commit (the chord changes what A-D offer; a subsequent single tap commits). Test: mis-stomp a chord — nothing the audience hears may change.

**Why:** Chords are the ergonomic failure mode of every low-switch device, and on this unit E and F are literally chords, not hardware.

*Evidence:* All three HeadRush manuals open the Basic Operations section with the same bolded warning that chain order 'is not necessarily reflected in the footswitches'. Anagram FAQ #381: 'the footswitches are close together if you have large/wide shoes… This can result in the Bank Change being inadvertently triggered', and it cannot be disabled. A Fractal user caught FS3+FS4 reaching for a scene, opened the tuner and muted himself going into a solo. A Helix owner: 'I tend to be a bit of a bumble foot' — the community fix was building a wooden cover over the bank switches. HX Stomp gets it right: FS1+FS2 QUEUES the previous bank; a later single tap commits.

### 10. Draw the binding on the thing it controls, not in a settings screen

**Rule:** Any parameter driven by the expression pedal or a switch shows a badge (EXP / A / B) inline, its assigned window shaded on the slider track, and the live controller position riding it. A separate global list may exist as an index, never as the only answer. Test: open any parameter — can you tell what owns it without navigating?

**Why:** 'What is my pedal doing right now' is the question every product in this survey fails, and it's a memory problem only because nobody drew the answer where the user already was.

*Evidence:* Line 6 Stadium's best single detail: a controlled parameter renders white with draggable min/max nodes on its own slider; crossing min past max reverses it. Fractal marks every modifiable parameter with a yellow dot BEFORE you try. HeadRush draws a lock icon on parameters the treadle exclusively owns and a pedal icon on shared ones. Dimehead's Room knob shows 'a small knob symbol… that this parameter is currently driven by the room knob'. Against: Quad Cortex has NO global expression view — users hunt block by block — and its ranges are in % while parameters read in dB/ms/Hz, producing the documented 'EQ output at -5 dB, what percentage is that?' dead end.

### 11. Resolve binding conflicts out loud, at bind time

**Rule:** Binding a switch that already does something lists what it does and offers Replace / Add / Cancel. Switches already bound are marked in any picker. New gear auto-binds to the first free switch (with a toast), but the amp and IR rows never auto-bind. Test: bind two actions to switch A — the second attempt must show the first.

**Why:** Silent stacking produces a switch that behaves oddly weeks later, on stage, with no trail back to the cause.

*Evidence:* Line 6 forum: a user's switches 'needed a double press to work'; diagnosis was a Command Center command stacked onto a footswitch that already held a stomp assignment, and the fix required the desktop editor. FabFilter states plainly: 'There is no warning when you associate a different knob with a controller number that is already used. It will just be replaced.' Ableton silently removes the first mapping when a parameter is remapped. Morningstar users are still requesting that already-used CC numbers be greyed out. The auto-assign half is Helix's 'Assign FX to Stomps', which explicitly never auto-assigns Amp, Preamp, Power Amp, Cab, IR, Split or Merge — plus HeadRush's 'Auto Assign Footswitches' toggle.

### 12. Arm the drag; never let swipe and reorder be the same gesture

**Rule:** Reordering is either an explicit affordance (↑/↓ buttons, a grip handle, a Reorder sheet) or a press-and-hold ≥300ms with haptic arm — never a bare drag on a scrollable surface. Test: swipe fast across the chain twenty times and count how many times something moved.

**Why:** On a phone, one-handed, in the dark, a mis-drag that silently reorders your rig is the single most destructive touch failure available — and this engine makes reorder audible, so it doesn't even fail quietly.

*Evidence:* Darkglass Anagram's most-cited defect: 'you will never miss dragging a block, but successful scrolling is more of a lottery'… 'the situation is even worse in the preset manager: each time I try to scroll through the list of presets, I'm moving a preset to another location inadvertently.' No undo; the only recovery is switching preset away and back. Darkglass confirmed 4 Feb 2026 'It's in our backlog' and it was still unfixed at v1.17.1 six months later. Guitar World hit it too. HeadRush's delete gesture (tap-hold-drag-to-the-bottom-bar) is a two-handed gesture that would misfire one-handed. The repo's existing ↑/↓ reorder in StackEditView is already the right answer — this principle says keep it.

### 13. No primary verb behind a hidden gesture

**Rule:** Every action reachable by double-tap, long-press or swipe must also be reachable from a visible control. Gestures are accelerators only. Test: turn off all gesture knowledge and complete every task using visible affordances.

**Why:** A hidden gesture is not a feature; it's a feature the user doesn't have.

*Evidence:* Engadget on the Positive Grid Spark app: 'The signal chain relies heavily on double taps… And there's no indication in the UI that double tapping is how you change out amps and effects.' MusicRadar on QC mini: 'there is no visual indication that gesturing upward brings in Gig View, gesturing down reveals the I/O settings' — QC added boot tooltips in CorOS 4.0.0, five years after launch, and footswitch access to Gig View in the same release. Anagram's encoder 'back' gesture (press, rotate left, release) had to be added as an opt-in SETTING because a single-button encoder has no natural back. NAM Live gets the balance right: single tap auditions, double tap loads — but Load also exists as a button. Also: 'pinch to zoom the chain' is a myth — two independent searches found zero occurrences across four HeadRush manuals and all QC docs. Don't build it.

### 14. Numbers in real units, always on screen, never inferred

**Rule:** Every continuous control shows its exact value and unit next to it; every expression/MIDI range is expressed in the target parameter's units ('-12.0 dB → 0.0 dB'), never in percent or 0-127. Test: point at any slider — the number and its unit must already be visible without touching it.

**Why:** Unit-free controls push arithmetic onto the user at exactly the moment they can least do it.

*Evidence:* Dimehead's own official tutorial has the presenter computing on camera: 'I could crank out a calculator and check what is 80% of 128, so that would be round about 102… not exactly sure because we're not seeing the actual value.' Quad Cortex expresses expression ranges as 0-100% while parameters read in dB/ms/Hz — a forum user with an EQ output at -5 dB could not work out what MAX RANGE to set. Anagram's open wish list literally contains 'Show knob values in block icon' and 'include a label for the frequency'. Anagram also proves the precision half: 'it takes a solid five minutes of turning and praying to hit exactly 70.0Hz on the dang HPF' — they disabled touch on continuous parameters in v1.14 and only added a numeric keypad in v1.16, a year after launch. On touch, ship all three: distance-based fine drag, double-tap to reset, tap-the-value for a keypad.

### 15. Offline is the floor, and backgrounding is a test case

**Rule:** Every rig, model and IR loads, edits and switches in airplane mode; TONE3000 browse is strictly additive. The BLE/USB MIDI link and the audio session must survive app backgrounding, screen lock and an incoming call, and reconnect with no user ritual. Test: airplane mode + cold start + switch to another app and back = zero degradation.

**Why:** This is the only failure in the corpus that produced an unrecoverable on-stage story, and it is entirely self-inflicted.

*Evidence:* Cortex Cloud would not LAUNCH without an internet connection: an App Store reviewer at a festival in a wooded area 'couldn't change any of my sounds', calling it 'inexcusable and absolutely insane'; the app sits at 3.4/5. Another: 'whenever I switch to another app and come back, it says I need an internet connection (even though I have one)… causing a lot of friction during rehearsals'. Offline Mode (v2.8.0, Nov 2025) only covers device settings. Connection fragility is the sibling failure: Cortex Cloud 'loses connection and requires unplugging the device to reconnect' after switching to a browser; TONEX Control 'screen freeze… it doesn't let me switch/save presets'. Also aligns with CLAUDE.md's TONE3000-parity rule: the free, offline core is the product.

### 16. Lock the glass, keep the foot alive — but never lock away the safety controls

**Rule:** A perform lock that ignores all touch except a deliberate multi-step unlock, while footswitches stay fully live. Mute/panic, master volume and the tuner remain reachable while locked. Keep-screen-on and an in-app brightness override are stage requirements, not settings polish. Test: lock it, put it in a pocket, stomp — the rig must change and nothing else may.

**Why:** A phone on a mic-stand clamp or a pedalboard is the worst accidental-touch surface in this entire survey, and locking is also the cheapest route to big dark-stage targets.

*Evidence:* Quad Cortex added LOCK (power button → overlay, locks touchscreen AND master volume) after shipping, explicitly as a response to accidental touches during shows; MusicRadar frames the large panel as 'a double-edged sword if you hit the wrong thing while playing a show', and guitar.com says the QC mini's layout 'does invite an accidental step on the screen'. HeadRush ships a dedicated Lock Screen showing only rig name, setlist name and a user logo, exiting in three taps. Dimehead's Stage Mode deactivates the pots entirely and (v1.0.0) restores itself on startup. The mistake to avoid is Anagram's: its Lock Mode also kills the long-press route to the Mixer and Global EQ, and does NOT prevent the accidental two-footswitch bank change.

### 17. Auto-persist, one undo, and no performance gesture may discard edits

**Rule:** Edits persist to the rig immediately with a per-item dirty marker; offer Revert and Duplicate instead of Save/Save-as; keep one level of undo in the chain editor. No tuner, mute, lock or footswitch action may reload state and drop unsaved edits. Test: edit a value, hit the tuner, exit — the edit is still there.

**Why:** Every product in this survey lacks undo, every one of their forums has a thread requesting it, and the destructive-side-effect bug is the specific class that only bites on stage.

*Evidence:* Dimehead: long-pressing into the tuner reloads the preset and silently reverts unsaved edits — 'my gain reverts back to 2.0 which was there before. That is unfortunately a bug.' Nano Cortex: hold-SAVE 'will overwrite the currently loaded Preset', and the documented workaround is 'use the app instead'. Quad Cortex's only unsaved cue is an italicised preset name; guitarguitar calls saving 'a little backward and convoluted' and reports losing patches. Anagram has neither undo nor an 'Are you sure?' and both are open wish-list items. Steal Dimehead's clean half (a SAVE badge appears only when dirty; switching away discards) and Nano Cortex's per-slot white dot showing WHICH block changed. Also copy Anagram v1.3.0's nuance: don't mark a rig dirty merely because the user switched away and back.

### 18. Browsing must never touch the audio graph; audition ≠ commit

**Rule:** Scrolling a model or IR list does zero audio-thread work. One tap auditions (through the existing DI/pre-render path), an explicit commit loads. Test: flick a 500-item model list for ten seconds while playing — no dropout, no load.

**Why:** With one model and one IR, the picker is the most-used screen in the app, and the naive implementation puts a file load behind a scroll gesture.

*Evidence:* Darkglass KosmOS v1.11.1 had to ship a fix for 'audio backend crash when scrolling through cabinet files or neural models'. Auditioning captures before download was a years-long Nano Cortex request — 'it takes a lot of extra steps just to listen to any capture' — only shipped in NanOS 2.2.0; before that the loop was download → load into a preset → listen → delete. HeadRush's cloud browser has a play icon to 'preview a rig's sound without downloading it', and v5.1 extends it to 500,000+ TONE3000 captures. NAM Live: 'One tap auditions a tone on your rig; a double-tap loads it.' The repo already has the audition pre-render and the DI tray — this is wiring, not invention.

### 19. Show the headroom before it becomes a dropout, and level-match every tone

**Rule:** Surface live DSP/thermal headroom in the editor, offer the honest quality trade (A2 Full vs Lite/Feather, IR length) as a user choice, and give every rig an output level with always-visible input/output meters. Test: is there anywhere on screen that tells you how close you are to the ceiling, and can two rigs be level-matched without leaving the rig?

**Why:** On a phone with thermal throttling this is not optional, and a rig that jumps in volume gets blamed on the amp model rather than the app.

*Evidence:* HeadRush puts a CPU meter in the corner and says plainly that 100% means 'popping, clicks, etc.', then offers the explicit cheap tier (IR truncated to 1024 vs 2048). Anagram added a DSP-overload popup (v1.6.0) and opt-in DSP load meters (v1.16.0), and publishes safe simultaneous NAM counts. The anti-pattern is Quad Cortex, which silently disables Global EQ and Input Gate when a preset exceeds resources. On levels: Fractal stores preset MAIN output level per scene, Helix has per-snapshot Output Level, and Anagram's most-filed tone complaint is 'sounded great at home on headphones, but at the gig they sounded woofy/boomy/muddy'. Dimehead's dBu→gain lookup table in its FAQ is the cautionary tale — the fix is to analyse the model on import and pre-fill a suggested trim, which is exactly what Dimehead's own IMPORT dialog does right.


## Adversarial critique

**Verdict:** Strong research, wrong foundation, and it does not know this codebase as well as it thinks it does. The corpus work is genuinely good — the vendor failure taxonomy (Anagram's drag-vs-scroll, QC's dark-stage LEDs, Dimehead's tuner reverting edits, Cortex Cloud's offline lockout) is the most useful part of the document and should be kept verbatim as a regression checklist. But the load-bearing premise is false. The doctrine asserts that with ONE model + ONE IR "EVERY audible change is a model swap" and "there is no cheap layer to hide behind," and on that basis kills scenes (principle 2) and justifies principle 1. Source/dsp/ToneEngine.h contradicts it: gate, IR enable, 3-band EQ, delay, reverb, in-gain and out-gain are all RT-safe, instant, audible and bypassable — and delay and reverb are not exposed in the mobile UI at all. The doctrine confused `StackModel`'s inert chain ROWS with the engine's real fixed NODES. Two of the nineteen principles collapse on that alone, and the most valuable foot action available on this rig — an instant solo boost via `setOutputDb`, already RT-safe and already used by the mute path — appears nowhere in the document. Second problem: roughly a quarter of the principles restate what the repo shipped on 2026-08-16/19 (learn-only binding, live monitor, per-binding FirePolicy, browsing off the audio graph, explicit reorder buttons) with nine vendors of evidence attached, while the genuinely hard, repo-specific constraints go unmentioned — that HID keyboard mode has no key-up so "hold" cannot be detected on the pedal as it ships; that screen lock kills the HID transport, making principle 15's own test unpassable; that dispatch runs on the paint-contended message thread; that a failed load wedges rig switching for 30 seconds; that `Stack` carries no engine parameters at all, so rigs cannot be level-matched even in principle; that the 6-slot physical mirror and learn-only capture are mutually exclusive as written; and that the pedal can change bank under you and make every binding fire the wrong action silently. Third problem: it contradicts itself in three places and never notices — bindings are simultaneously "global by default" and "an explicit per-rig table"; principle 7's permanent mirror fights principle 15's lock, and both fight CLAUDE.md's "bottom nav functional on every screen"; principle 10 offers an "Add" that `ControlMap` deliberately refuses to persist. Recommendation: keep the antipattern corpus and open questions 1, 4, 5 and 10 (those four are the real unknowns and two of them are one adb session away from being answered). Rewrite principle 1's taxonomy as a compile-time attribute of `ControlAction`. Delete principle 2 and replace it with "snapshots flip the ENGINE nodes, not the chain rows" — which is buildable this week. Cut principles 12 and 17 as already-true. Before any of it, resolve two things in code, not prose: put engine parameters into the `Stack` model, and instrument the stomp-to-engine path end to end. Until those two land, this is a specification for a different app than the one in this repository.


### Gaps

- NO SOLO BOOST. The single most-used foot action in a one-amp rig is +3 dB for the solo, and on this engine it is FREE and INSTANT (`ToneEngine::setOutputDb`, already RT-safe, already used by the mute path in /Users/chris.harper/Development/nam_app/Source/app/ui/AppShellControls.cpp). It is not in `ControlAction`, not in any of the three named layouts, and not in the doctrine. A gigging player will look for it before they look for scenes.
- TUNER != MUTE IS NEVER SPECIFIED. `ControlAction::TunerToggle` and `ControlAction::OutputMute` are separate verbs, and the layouts spend switches on 'D = tuner/mute' as if they were one thing. Silent tuning is table stakes on every tuner pedal ever built. Nineteen principles and not one sentence saying that entering the tuner mutes the output.
- MUTE ACROSS A RIG CHANGE IS UNDEFINED. You are muted, you stomp to the next rig — do you come back muted or live? Both answers are stage-fatal in different ways. `setControlMute` stores `preMuteOutputDb_` on AppShell, entirely outside the rig model, so today the answer is emergent, not designed.
- NO PANIC. Principle 15 name-checks 'mute/panic' and then no panic action exists, and the doctrine never defines what panic means on a phone: kill output, bypass the chain, drop the model, reset the audio device? A player who hears feedback at 110 dB needs one switch with one unambiguous meaning.
- NO RENAME, STILL. AppShellStacks.cpp:143 says it in a comment: 'there is no rename UI anywhere in the app'. There is no `juce::TextEditor` anywhere in Source/app/ui. Every principle about reading the rig name at five feet is moot when the names are 'Stack 3' or a 34-character TONE3000 title the user cannot shorten. The doctrine buries this in a parenthetical in mobileChainDesign instead of making it the precondition for principle 6.
- NO GIG-DAY PREFLIGHT. 'Are all four rigs' models actually resident on this phone, is the battery going to hold 45 minutes, is the interface claimed, is the pedal connected' is the screen a gigging player actually wants, and it appears only as a subordinate clause inside the ALIASES-OR-COPIES open question.
- END-TO-END FOOT LATENCY IS NEVER BUDGETED. The doctrine measures only the model swap. The player experiences stomp -> BLE -> Android input -> JUCE MIDI thread -> AsyncUpdater -> message thread (shared with every repaint) -> load -> engine -> 14-16 ms round trip. Only the middle segment is even named.
- WHAT THE AUDIENCE HEARS DURING A LOAD IS LEFT AS PHILOSOPHY. `ToneEngine::render` is explicitly documented as callable with model==null. Whether that is silence, dry passthrough, or the retired model's tail is a concrete, answerable, testable question in this repo today, independent of whether the crossfade in US 11,423,870 B2 ever gets built. The doctrine deferred an answerable question to an open question.
- NO SETLIST, BUT THE FOOT GRAMMAR ALREADY ASSUMES ONE. Layout 1 says 'A/B/C/D = direct-select the four rigs pinned to the current page' and 'E = page down' — a page/bank concept that only a setlist primitive provides, which is itself open question 11. The footswitch design has an undeclared dependency on an unresolved question, and no `ControlAction` for direct-select-rig-N exists.
- NO VOLUME-PEDAL ROLE. The most common expression assignment in the world is a volume pedal, this engine has an instant RT-safe out-gain to drive it, and the expression section discusses curves, damping and auto-engage without ever naming the one assignment 90% of users will make first.
- NO STORY FOR THE INTERFACE OR THE CABLE. controllers.md records that the iRig HD X drains the phone and blocks the USB-C port; realtime-audio.md records an AAudio watchdog that rescans ~2 s after a device dies. Nineteen principles, zero about what the player sees when the interface is yanked, the hub browns out, or the app comes back on the wrong input mid-set.
- NO BUILD ORDER. Nineteen principles, twelve open questions, no sequencing and no definition of the minimum shippable foot-control set. At least three principles (scenes, crossfade, the 6-slot mirror) cannot be started until an open question is answered, and the doctrine never says which three.

### Weak principles

- PRINCIPLE 2, 'A scene layer with nothing audible under it is a lie' — THE PREMISE IS FACTUALLY WRONG. /Users/chris.harper/Development/nam_app/Source/dsp/ToneEngine.h exposes noise gate (enable + threshold), IR enable, 3-band EQ, delay (enable/time/feedback/mix), reverb (enable/room/damping/mix), in-gain and out-gain — every one an RT-safe setter, instant, audible and bypassable. Delay and reverb are not exposed in the mobile UI AT ALL (only Source/app/MainComponent.cpp, the desktop host). The doctrine conflated stacks.md's 'pedal/post items are stored + visual only' — which is about `ChainItem` rows in `StackModel` — with the engine's own fixed DSP nodes, which are real. A snapshot that flips delay on, opens the gate and adds +3 dB out is fully audible today with zero model loads. The principle's own test ('point at every control and name the audio-thread parameter it moves') passes for six controls that already exist. Delete this principle or rewrite it as 'scenes must flip the engine nodes, not the chain rows'.
- PRINCIPLE 1's 'why' INHERITS THE SAME ERROR. 'EVERY audible change is a model swap... there is currently no cheap layer to hide behind' is false for the reason above. The INSTANT/LOADING taxonomy is the best idea in the document; its justification is wrong and its enforcement is the weakest available — a label a reviewer eyeballs in a sheet. Class is a property of `ControlAction`, so make it a compile-time attribute of the enum with a test that every action declares one, not a UI string.
- PRINCIPLE 3's TEST IS UNFALSIFIABLE AND AIMED AT THE WRONG COST. 'Stomp A; the action must fire before your foot leaves the switch' cannot be judged by feel. Meanwhile the real millisecond cost in THIS codebase is unmeasured and unmentioned: `MidiControl` queues on the JUCE MIDI thread and drains via `AsyncUpdater` on the MESSAGE thread — the same thread painting every scrolling list and every blurred card backdrop. The doctrine budgets press-vs-release (~50-100 ms of vendor anecdote) and ignores an unbounded, paint-contended hop in its own repo. The test should be instrumented timestamps from event arrival to engine write.
- PRINCIPLES 4 AND 5 ARE ALREADY SHIPPED, DRESSED AS DISCOVERY. Learn-only binding, the live monitor, and per-binding FirePolicy all landed 2026-08-16/19 (`ControllersScreen::onCyclePolicy`, `MidiControl::onEvent`, `ControlMap::beginLearn`). Both stated tests pass today. Nine vendors of evidence for a decision the repo made and implemented months ago is filler, and it crowds out the parts of those areas that are NOT solved (see missing constraints).
- PRINCIPLE 6's ARCMINUTE MATH IS FALSE PRECISION AND SELF-CONTRADICTORY. ANSI/HFES legibility figures are for seated operators viewing a console at normal incidence; a phone at boot height is viewed obliquely and off-axis, where contrast collapses before size does. Worse, the arithmetic doesn't close: >=64 dp caps on a 360 dp-wide screen is roughly five to six characters, so the principle mandates a type size that cannot render the names the app actually produces — and there is no rename UI, so the user cannot shorten them. It also collides with itself: it sizes for five-foot legibility while principle 7 makes those same tiles tap-to-fire, which requires arm's reach. Pick one: readable from the mic stand, or operable from the mic stand.
- PRINCIPLE 7 ASSERTS CLAUDE.md COMPLIANCE INSTEAD OF ARGUING IT. 'Satisfies CLAUDE.md's rule that the bottom chrome is the only persistent chrome' is stated, not shown, for a map described as 'persistent' and 'mandatory, permanent'. And it collides head-on with principle 15: a lock that 'ignores all touch except a deliberate multi-step unlock' makes the bottom nav non-functional, which CLAUDE.md forbids in the same breath it forbids second chrome ('visible and functional on EVERY screen'). Two principles, direct collision, unresolved — and this is exactly the class of thing CLAUDE.md says goes in decisions.md rather than being decided locally.
- PRINCIPLE 10's 'Replace / Add / Cancel' CONTRADICTS THE SHIPPED MODEL AND DOESN'T KNOW IT. `ControlMap::bind` is documented 1:1 in BOTH directions: 'two switches racing one action, or one switch firing two actions, are both user mistakes we refuse to persist'. 'Add' is not a button to offer, it is a model change with consequences the doctrine never works out (dispatch order? what does the 6-slot mirror render for a stacked switch?). And 'new gear auto-binds to the first free switch' is IMPOSSIBLE under learn-only capture: the app knows only signatures it has seen, so it has no inventory of switches and therefore no concept of a free one. Two of this principle's three clauses cannot be built as written.
- PRINCIPLE 16's UNDO IS ASSERTED WITH NO MECHANISM, AND IS ITSELF A 'LOADING' ACTION. stacks.md: 'Editing is audible' — every reorder, gear swap and channel change applies to the engine immediately. So by principle 1's own taxonomy, undoing a reorder is a LOADING action. An undo that costs a model reload mid-song is not a safety net, it is a second mistake. The principle needs to say which edits are undoable (the instant ones) and which are not.
- PRINCIPLE 18's 'A2 Full vs Lite/Feather as a user choice' IS A CATEGORY ERROR. Architecture is baked into the .nam file the user downloaded from TONE3000; there is no runtime quality tier to offer. IR truncation (2048 -> 1024) is real and worth shipping; the model tier is not a knob that exists. Also, the DSP-headroom half is already half-built and uncredited: `ToneEngine::cpuLoad()` and `overCapacityCount()` exist and are unsurfaced — the doctrine cites HeadRush's CPU meter instead of the repo's own telemetry.
- PRINCIPLES 12 AND 17 ARE UNIVERSAL HIG ADVICE WITH VENDOR ANECDOTES STAPLED ON. 'No primary verb behind a hidden gesture' is already true here — reorder is +/- buttons, there are almost no gestures to audit. 'Browsing must never touch the audio graph' is already the architecture (PlayScreen never fetches; audition is a pre-render; one in-flight load with two pending slots). Neither changes a single line of this product. They read as corpus padding.
- PRINCIPLE 8's QUEUE SEMANTICS ASSUME THE APP OWNS THE CHORD. It does not. chocolate-plus.md: 'Bank changes are also a simultaneous left/right press' — on this unit, a two-switch press may be consumed by the pedal's own firmware and change what A-D send, without the app ever seeing a chord event. 'Make combo actions QUEUE rather than commit' is unimplementable for a chord the app cannot observe. Open question 4 asks what E and F send but the principle is written as though the answer were already 'a distinct app-visible message'.
- PRINCIPLE 14 GIVES NO PRECISION RULE, WHICH IS WHERE UNIT DISPLAY ACTUALLY FAILS. '-12.0 dB' vs '-12 dB', resolution while dragging, rounding at the endpoints, and what a 0.1 dB readout does to a 56 dp row at perform type size are the real decisions. The principle also scopes itself as 'always on screen', which collides with principle 6's '<=6 objects, no thin strokes' unless someone states that these are two different screens with two different rules.

### Missing repo constraints

- HID KEYBOARD MODE HAS NO KEY-UP, SO 'HOLD' CANNOT BE DETECTED AT ALL. decisions.md 2026-08-19: 'Keys are DISCRETE like program changes -- JUCE surfaces only keyPressed, there is no release -- so both bypass edge detection via isDiscreteKind'. Chris's pedal SHIPS in this mode. The doctrine mandates 'exactly ONE hold in the whole product (tuner/mute)' and it is unimplementable on the actual hardware as it arrives. Same fact kills principle 5 for the primary transport: the three-way FirePolicy control is a dead control on `ControlKind::Key`, because there is no release half to interpret. The doctrine cites auto-repeat as a reason to make hold idempotent and never notices hold is undetectable.
- SCREEN LOCK KILLS THE HID TRANSPORT OUTRIGHT. Key events go to the Android lock screen, not to a backgrounded JUCE app. Principle 15's test — 'lock it, put it in a pocket, stomp — the rig must change' — cannot pass in keyboard mode at all. BLE MIDI in a foreground service might; HID cannot. Open question 10 asks about Doze and BLE connection parameters and never separates the two transports, which is the whole answer.
- SOFT KEYBOARD vs BLE HID KEYBOARD ON THE SAME INPUT FOCUS. There is no `juce::TextEditor` anywhere in Source/app/ui — this app has never built text or numeric entry. Two doctrine items require it (rig rename, 'tap-the-value for a keypad'). On Android a connected physical keyboard suppresses the soft keyboard, and any focused field consumes keystrokes: renaming a rig with the pedal paired means the pedal types into the field, or the soft keyboard never appears. That is a broken interaction nobody has priced, and it is created by the doctrine's own two demands.
- NO CONTINUOUS CONTROL LAYER EXISTS. `ControlEvent` is discrete; `ControlAction` is a closed enum of eight verbs (RigNext/Prev, ToneNext/Prev, TunerToggle, OutputMute, ChainBypass, RigCompare). Expression needs a new event kind, a parameter-addressing scheme (which parameter of which node, keyed to survive a rig edit), per-binding range/curve/damping/auto-engage state, persistence, and RT-safe writes. ~1500 words of expression UI sit on a model layer that does not exist, while other sections say 'this is wiring, not invention'. The asymmetry makes expression read as comparably cheap. It is the largest single piece of new model work in the document.
- THE ON-SCREEN MIRROR NEEDS A SIGNATURE -> PHYSICAL-POSITION MAP THAT LEARN-ONLY CANNOT PRODUCE. You cannot draw 'A B C D in the controller's physical order' from captured CC numbers and keycodes. Either ship a guided ritual ('stomp the leftmost switch... now the next one') or drop the geometry claim. Principle 4 (learn only, never a table) and principle 7 (render the pedal's physical geometry) are both stated as absolutes and they are mutually exclusive as written.
- ENGINE PARAMETERS ARE NOT PART OF A RIG. `Stack` is {uid, name, chain, extra} — no level, gain, EQ, gate, delay or reverb (Source/model/StackModel.h:44). The Play-card quick sliders write in-gain, EQ low/mid/high and gate threshold straight to the global engine (AppShell.cpp:276). Change rigs and your gain and EQ stay wherever you left them. Consequence: principle 18's 'give every rig an output level' is not merely unbuilt, it is impossible without a model change; two rigs cannot be level-matched; and 'the rig is the performance unit' is false at the data layer. This should be the first constraint in the document, not a clause in the eighteenth principle.
- THE FAILURE PATH IS A 30-SECOND STAGE HOLE. `startToneLoad` permits one in-flight load with two pending slots and arms a 30 s `ApplyTimeout` watchdog (AppShellStackApply.cpp). A hung or failed load wedges rig switching for up to half a minute. The doctrine writes a whole principle about the load STATE and nothing about the failure DURATION. A stage-appropriate rule is a 1-2 s budget, keep the currently-live model, and report after the song — the opposite of what is shipped.
- THE OFFLINE FLOOR IS ASSERTED AGAINST A NETWORK-FIRST APPLY PATH. Principle 19 says every rig, model and IR loads in airplane mode. In code, `findLocalEntry` only matches legacy wizard-created `LibraryEntry` filenames — and stacks.md says 'nothing creates new ones anymore'. Every item the EDIT picker creates is a TONE3000 id routed through `svc_.loadTone`. Whether that route is cache-first is nowhere stated. The doctrine's best principle is written as if it were already true.
- FOOT EVENTS ALREADY NAVIGATE THE APP, AGAINST THE DOCTRINE'S OWN RULE. `runControlAction`: RigNext/RigPrev call `openStackDetail()`; TunerToggle calls `show(Screen::Play)` first. The footswitchDesign says 'Never let a foot event navigate the app away from the perform surface as a side effect (QC's banking used to eject players out of Gig View)' and never audits the shipped dispatch that does exactly that. Deeper: `stepStack` reads `stacksDetail_->currentIndex()`, so 'which rig is live' is a property of a SCREEN, not of the model — which is why a stomp has to open a screen at all.
- THE PEDAL CAN REMAP ITSELF MID-SET AND THE APP CANNOT SEE IT. The Chocolate has 16 groups / 64 banks and a three-digit display, and a bank change is itself a simultaneous press — the same accidental chord principle 8 worries about. After a bank change A/B/C/D send DIFFERENT CCs. With channel-agnostic wildcard matching, bindings do not go dead cleanly; they fire the wrong action or nothing, with no trail back to the cause. The doctrine has a whole principle on binding conflicts and nothing on the controller silently reconfiguring itself. The cheap mitigation — warn when an unbound signature arrives repeatedly ('this switch isn't bound; did the pedal change bank?') — is unmentioned.
- EXPRESSION MAY REQUIRE THE VENDOR APP, WHICH BREAKS THE DOCTRINE'S OWN PROMISE. The unit ships in HID keyboard mode; the whole expression design assumes CCs arrive. Open question 5 frames this as a jack-sharing question (TRS is MIDI out AND expression in). The real question is a MODE question: if switches work today only because they arrive as keystrokes, the expression jack's CC may not reach the app at all without flipping the unit to MIDI mode in CubeSuite — the one thing controllers.md swears users will never have to install. Nothing in the document notices that 'never require CubeSuite' and 'ship expression' may be mutually exclusive on this specific unit.
- THE PHONE IS THE AMP, THE INTERFACE, THE DISPLAY AND THE BLE HOST, SIMULTANEOUSLY, FOR 45 MINUTES. NAM A2 inference under RelWithDebInfo, a 192-frame buffer, screen on at stage brightness, BLE radio active, and (per controllers.md) an iRig HD X that drains the battery and blocks the charging port unless a powered hub is in the chain. Thermal throttling is named once, in principle 18, as a meter. There is no principle about degradation strategy when the phone throttles, no rule about what the app drops first, and no acknowledgement that the doctrine's own 'keep-screen-on + brightness override' requirement makes the thermal problem worse.
- ACCESSIBILITY IS QUOTED AS AN ANTIPATTERN AND THEN OMITTED AS A PRINCIPLE. The corpus includes Cortex Mobile's 'totally inaccessible for screen readers' 1-star, and the doctrine draws no rule from it. Every screen here is hand-painted with manual hit rects (`ControllersScreen::rowRects_`, `mouseDown` hit-testing) — there is no accessibility tree at all. On a product whose whole thesis is 'readable and operable under bad conditions', that is a conspicuous hole.
- THE MESSAGE THREAD IS THE FOOT-CONTROL BOTTLENECK AND IS NEVER NAMED. MIDI arrives on a JUCE MIDI thread, is queued under a lock, and drained by `AsyncUpdater` on the message thread — coalesced, and sharing that thread with every repaint in the app, including the blurred card backdrops and scrolling decks. Whatever the .nam swap costs, the doctrine's entire 'fire on the first event' argument is decided upstream of it, in a queue nobody has instrumented.

## Design guidance extracted


### mobileChainDesign

CONSTRAINT FIRST: the engine is ONE model + ONE IR and pedal/post rows have no audio node. So the chain editor's job is not routing — it is (a) making the two audible slots unmistakable, (b) letting you swap what's in them, and (c) never lying about the rest. This differs from every product in the survey and should be designed for, not around.

LAYOUT — vertical list, never a pedalboard, never horizontal.

1. ONE functional header (CLAUDE.md allows exactly one), non-scrolling, ~96dp: rig name (editable — there is still no rename UI anywhere in the app, and that's a gap), then a single "AUDIBLE NOW" line reading `model name → IR name`, then the input/output meters pinned to the left and right edges of that row. Anagram pins meters in every view and mode and owners cite them as the practical unity-gain tool; Dimehead pins them to the screen edges too. No brand wordmark, no subtitle blurb.

2. The chain as a vertical scrolling list of full-width rows, 80-88dp tall — the existing StackEditView shape, kept. Row anatomy: 64dp thumbnail left; title + format + (for amps) active channel centre; right edge carries a small ⋮ and the ↑/↓ reorder buttons. Signal flows top-to-bottom.

3. TWO ROWS ARE STRUCTURALLY DIFFERENT AND MUST LOOK IT. The active AMP row and the active CAB row are the audible spine. Give them a filled accent left-bar plus an "AUDIBLE" chip. Every other row (pedal/post) carries a muted "STORED" chip and, in the item sheet, one honest line: "Saved with this rig — no audio node yet." Nano Cortex's fixed chain is a UX asset (stable positions, no drag to fight one-handed); its mistake was the arbitrary slot-TYPE allow-list nobody could predict, so if the DSP ever imposes a constraint, state the reason inline where the user hits it.

4. NO HORIZONTAL SCROLLING OF THE CHAIN, EVER. Nothing runs off the right edge (BIAS FX 2's failure), nothing needs zoom (Anagram had to add a zoomed-out view at v1.8), and there is nothing to pinch (pinch is a myth in this category — zero occurrences across four HeadRush manuals and all QC docs).

5. REORDER IS EXPLICIT. Keep ↑/↓ buttons. Do not add drag-on-swipe (Anagram's worst defect). If a drag affordance is ever wanted, it must be an explicit grip handle on the right where the thumb is, requiring a ≥300ms press-and-hold with a haptic arm. Long-press on a row = a context menu (Duplicate / Remove), not a drag — HeadRush's tap-hold-drag-to-the-bin is a two-handed gesture that will misfire one-handed in the dark.

6. REORDER IS AUDIBLE HERE, SO SAY SO. Because moving a row can change which amp/cab is live, any reorder that changes the audible pair must confirm what it did with an inline, non-modal line: "Now audible: JCM800 → Twin Reverb", plus the load state while it applies. This is unique to this engine and no surveyed product has an analogue.

7. ITEM SHEET = one block per screen (the 4-inch Flex Prime is the cautionary tale — "the bass and treble controls aren't even on the same screen, because they simply won't fit"). Inside it: full-width horizontal bar sliders, NOT a row of five knobs. NUX MightyAmp's edit page is the model — big label left, value + unit right-aligned, ~56dp tall, roughly 5× the hit area of NAM Live's 5-across arc knobs at ~70dp each. Every slider gets: fixed-sensitivity drag (never speed-acceleration — QC's encoders fail on exactly this), distance-from-axis fine mode, double-tap to reset to default, and tap-the-number for a keypad.

8. A PINNED PERFORMANCE ROW. Let the user pin 4-6 parameters from anywhere in the rig (NAM input gain, output level, IR low-cut/high-cut, gate threshold) to a fixed-position row that never moves between rigs, so muscle memory works in the dark. This is Anagram's Bindings View reframed ("what will I want to tweak in real time?") plus Dimehead's Room-knob macro. It matters more on a phone than on hardware because there are no knobs to fall back on.

9. IR SHAPING IS A CURVE, NOT TWO NUMBER FIELDS. Give the cab row's sheet a live frequency-response plot with draggable low-cut/high-cut handles (Line 6 Stadium's Focus View for IRs; HeadRush's equivalent), with the numeric Hz values printed.

10. IN LISTS ELSEWHERE (Stacks home), never draw the chain — chip it. Each rig row gets a micro chip-strip (one tiny coloured chip per chain item, greyed for bypassed/stored), a left colour bar, and a badge showing which footswitch currently selects it (A/B/C/D). That's TONEX Control's preset list, and it answers "what's in this rig and which switch is it on" with no second screen.

11. DESIGN AND SCREENSHOT AT 360×640dp, not on the reference device — add a small-screen AVD to the existing E2E screenshot checklist. GE Labs' iPhone SE failure is what a fixed-height layout does on a short screen.

12. Model/IR picker gets first-class investment because with one model + one IR it IS the app: search-as-you-type, favourites, recents, full untruncated names at readable size, one-tap audition through the existing DI/pre-render path, explicit commit to load. Scrolling it must do zero audio-thread work.


### footswitchDesign

GRAMMAR (four physical switches A/B/C/D; E = A+B and F = C+D are CHORDS, not hardware).

CORE RULES
- Every switch carries exactly ONE action, fired on the first event that arrives (press / CC change / key-down). No double-tap anywhere in v1 — Morningstar documents that a double-tap assignment adds unavoidable latency to that switch's plain press, on every press, forever, regardless of any other setting.
- Exactly ONE hold in the whole product, and it must be idempotent and harmless: hold any switch = tuner/mute, exitable from any switch. Rationale specific to this repo: FirePolicy::Auto is documented to double-fire a momentary switch held past the 400ms window, and in HID keyboard mode Android auto-repeats a held key — so a hold must be an action you can fire twice with no consequence. Nothing the audience hears may live behind a hold.
- No action is reachable ONLY by a chord or a hold. Everything a foot can do, the screen can do (and vice versa) — Anagram's owner who concluded the tuner could only be exited "with an accurately placed finger" and refused to use it live is the failure mode.

DEFAULT MAP (all re-learnable; ship 3 NAMED LAYOUTS, not a free-form matrix — Nano Cortex's "4-Preset Mode / 2-Preset Mode" precedent)
- Layout 1 "4 rigs": A/B/C/D = direct-select the four rigs pinned to the current page. E = page down, F = page up.
- Layout 2 "3 rigs + tuner": A/B/C = rigs, D = tuner/mute, E/F = page.
- Layout 3 "2 rigs + A/B + mute": A/B = rigs, C = toggle-previous (instant A/B), D = mute, E/F = page.

CHORDS QUEUE, THEY DON'T COMMIT. E/F change which four rigs A-D offer and repaint the on-screen tiles; a subsequent single tap on A-D commits. A mis-stomped chord therefore changes nothing the audience hears. This is HX Stomp's bank-queue semantics, and it is the direct answer to Anagram FAQ #381 (wide shoes hitting two switches) and the Helix "bumble foot" thread. Chord detection also implies a debounce window before A alone can act — if the pedal sends E as a distinct message this cost is zero; if it sends A-then-B, make the window configurable with 0ms = chords disabled, and say in the UI which actions now cost latency.

BECAUSE EVERY COMMIT IS A MODEL LOAD (this engine's defining fact)
- A queued-but-uncommitted tile renders outlined; the committed one renders filled; a load in flight renders a progress fill on the tile AND in the header. Never pretend a load is instant.
- Re-selecting the currently live rig is a NO-OP by default (Fractal's "Ignore Redundant PC must be ON for seamless scene changes"), with "Toggle Previous" available as a setting (Helix's Snapshot Reselect) — that's the A/B rig action the repo already dispatches. A stray double-stomp must produce silence, not a rebuild.
- Never let a foot event navigate the app away from the perform surface as a side effect (QC's banking used to eject players out of Gig View).

ON-SCREEN MIRROR (mandatory, permanent)
Six slots laid out in the pedal's physical geometry: A B C D across one row, with E drawn as a bridge spanning A|B and F spanning C|D so the chord relationship teaches itself. Each slot shows label + colour + lit/dim + (when empty) "Tap to assign". Tapping a slot FIRES it — the screen is a soft copy of the pedalboard (HeadRush Core/Flex Prime, Helix Stadium non-XL Home>Play, QC Gig View). Bright = live, dim-but-present = not; never fully off (QC's dark-stage failure). Labels at perform-screen type size, readable at 5 ft.

BINDING UX
- Assignment is MIDI-learn only as the primary path: touch the target → "Listening — stomp the switch you want" → capture. A raw CC/keycode table exists as an advanced escape hatch. This is forced by the hardware: the Chocolate Plus's factory defaults are contested three ways and Chris's unit ships in HID keyboard mode.
- Each binding row shows the captured event verbatim plus a visible FirePolicy control (Trigger on press / Toggle / Momentary). Non-negotiable per the repo's own decision log.
- A live MIDI/key monitor is visible while learning, with a timestamp on the last event, so a pedal in the wrong mode is diagnosed in seconds rather than blamed on the app.
- Binding an already-bound switch lists what it currently does and offers Replace / Cancel — never silent replacement (FabFilter's stated behaviour, Ableton's silent unmapping, and the Helix "needs a double press" bug all argue this).
- Switch order is NOT chain order; bindings live in an explicit per-rig table, with drag-one-card-onto-another to SWAP two bindings (all three HeadRush manuals open with this warning in bold).
- New gear auto-binds to the first free switch with a toast; the amp and IR rows never auto-bind (Helix's "Assign FX to Stomps" excludes Amp/Preamp/Cab/IR by design). Provide the Manual global.
- Bindings are global by default (the pedal layout doesn't change per song); targets, ranges and curves live per rig, keyed by rig uid — and must survive a tone being re-downloaded or renamed, extending the dangling-uid guard already in Stacks.


### expressionDesign

ASSIGNMENT — from the target, never from a table.
Long-press the parameter (or tap it → "Assign") → sheet says "Listening — rock the pedal through its full range" → capture whatever CC arrives. Then, separately, ONE global Expression screen that lists every binding (source, target, range in units, curve, invert, unbind). Quad Cortex has no such page and its users hunt block by block; that omission is the single most-cited expression complaint in the corpus. Both surfaces, always.

CALIBRATION — a guided ritual with a live readout, not a settings field.
Copy QC exactly: "rock heel to toe" with a bar sweeping 0.00 → 1.00 and the raw CC value shown live. Store the OBSERVED min/max rather than assuming a 0-127 sweep. Then:
- Trim ~3% at each end by default (Fractal's community fix for pedals that calibrate but only reach 8-9 out of 10) and CLAMP so heel = exactly min and toe = exactly max. Darkglass shipped this broken three separate times (v1.14.0, v1.14.1, v1.16.3) — unit-test the endpoints.
- Fail loudly, not silently: "Pedal not detected" or "Range too narrow — only 41 of 127 values seen; check the cable" rather than doing nothing. This unit needs it: the repo records the Plus's 3.5mm TRS at 3.3V nominal and "less accommodating of some expression pedals" than the base Chocolate's 1/4". Mission Engineering's root cause is pot rotation vs rocker travel mismatch, producing exactly the dead-spots and premature-max signature.
- State the detected role of the TRS jack plainly (expression in vs MIDI out) — Nano Cortex's shared EXP/MIDI jack is a documented mode-trap where Morningstar had to flip it back and forth just to configure assignments.

RANGE, INVERSION, CURVE — per assignment, in the parameter's own units.
- Dual-handle range slider drawn ON THE PARAMETER's own track, shaded to show the assigned window, with the live pedal position riding it (Line 6 Stadium's white value + draggable min/max nodes is the best single detail in this survey).
- Values in real units: "-12.0 dB → 0.0 dB", never "0% → 100%". QC's %-vs-dB mismatch is a documented dead end; Dimehead's own demo does 0-127 arithmetic on camera.
- Inversion: allow crossing min past max (the hardware idiom) AND ship an explicit Invert toggle. Crossing is undiscoverable with a thumb; Ableton exposes inversion as a real command for this reason.
- Per-assignment curve — linear / log / exp. Anagram is linear-only and the standing request states the exact use case: "I need an expression pedal to work as a parametric pot in a volume mode and a linear pot in the wah mode."
- Per-assignment damping/slew (Fractal's Damping exists precisely to "cover up for a scratchy expression pedal" and kill zipper noise — expect both from a cheap pot into a 3.3V jack).

MULTI-TARGET — one pedal, several parameters, independent ranges.
Standard everywhere (Helix, QC, Fractal, Nano Cortex, Anagram), and MORE valuable with one fixed amp: a single sweep can back gain off while bringing output level up, which is the only "channel change" this engine can do without a reload. Show the count in the UI ("3 of 8 targets"), list them by name — never Helix's "MULTIPLE (X)" dead end — and error clearly BEFORE the ceiling, not after (Helix's "Too many controller assignments!" with no running count is a known irritation).

RECALL SEMANTICS — decide once, write it in controllers.md.
Steal Anagram v1.12.0 wholesale: expression values are NOT stored in rigs. On rig load, the parameter takes the CURRENT PHYSICAL PEDAL POSITION. This eliminates the classic "pedal is at toe but the rig recalled the heel value and the next nudge jumps" glitch. Separately, decide explicitly whether "current position" means raw hardware position or the mapped/scaled value — Morningstar shipped a bug where a PC+CC pair transmitted the raw 0-127 instead of the scaled value. Also offer pick-up takeover (Ableton's Pick-Up) so a newly created binding doesn't jump the volume.

BADGING — the answer to "what is my pedal doing right now" must be glanceable.
Any parameter owned by the pedal shows an EXP chip inline, its window shaded on the track, and — while the pedal is actively overriding — BOTH the stored value and the live value (Nano Cortex's LED-ring "stored value regardless of knob position", translated to touch). HeadRush's lock/pedal icon distinction is the same idea. Without this, discoverability is exactly where every surveyed product fails.

AUTO-ENGAGE — mandatory here, because there is no toe switch.
The Chocolate Plus's expression jack has no toe switch, so HeadRush's Classic wah-or-volume model is unavailable. Ship auto-engage instead: the assigned block engages on movement (Fractal's threshold — controller moves >5% within a 20ms window, specifically so a drooping pedal doesn't trigger it) and self-bypasses at a chosen position (heel / toe / stop) after a configurable Off Delay of 0-5000ms. Surface the off threshold as a visible number: disengage is exactly where Fractal users get stuck ("Both Speed and Position modes require you to manually move the pedal back beyond the Off Value threshold"). This buys back a footswitch on a four-switch controller, which is the whole argument.
Optional zero-switch gesture worth stealing (HeadRush v5.1): volume at 0% / heel-down auto-opens the tuner. Ship it defeatable.


### Open questions

- MEASURE THE GAP. No vendor in any of the nine reports publishes a millisecond figure for a preset/model change — all say 'split second', 'brief', 'noticeable' — and there is no published figure for a NAM A2 model swap at all. Every foot-control decision in this doctrine branches on whether swapping one .nam + one IR on Chris's phone costs 40ms or 1.5s. This must be measured on the real device before the MIDI spec is written, not assumed.
- BUILD THE CROSSFADE OR DECLARE TONE CHANGE A BETWEEN-SONGS ACTION? The reports disagree hard and both sides are defensible. HeadRush treats gapless spillover as its flagship feature and spends DSP on it; a Line 6 staffer counters that Helix's 40-120ms gap is the better trade and notes HeadRush's 'processor is completely taxed when you load two 2048 IR files'. Quad Cortex simply declares the gap architectural and tells users to restructure. The mechanism if we build it is US 11,423,870 B2 (both chains live, amplitude ramp, tail buffer), which extends the repo's existing publish-then-retire pattern — but it doubles peak model memory on a phone with thermal throttling.
- SHOULD SCENES EXIST AT ALL BEFORE AUDIBLE PEDAL DSP? Eight of nine reports say scenes/snapshots are the primary live unit. The repo already deleted scenes on 2026-08-15 because nothing they flip is audible. Both are right in their own context. The question is whether pedal nodes land before foot control ships — if they don't, the honest foot action list is exactly what's already dispatched (rig/tone next-prev, tuner, mute, chain bypass, A/B rig) and a scene UI would be theatre.
- WHAT DO E AND F ACTUALLY SEND ON THE WIRE? Still uncaptured per chocolate-plus.md ('Not yet captured: which keycodes this unit actually sends when stomped'). If E arrives as its own distinct message, chord latency is zero and the queue design is free. If E is literally A-then-B in quick succession, then A's plain press must be delayed by a debounce window — which is precisely the trade HeadRush had to ship a global setting for. The whole footswitch grammar's latency budget depends on this one observation.
- CAN THE PLUS CARRY EXPRESSION AND SWITCHES SIMULTANEOUSLY? Its 3.5mm TRS doubles as MIDI out AND expression in, and the reports flag the base Chocolate as either/or. If switches ride BLE while expression rides TRS, does the jack still accept a pedal? Nobody in the corpus answers this for the Plus specifically; Dimehead's tutorial documents the constraint on the base Chocolate.
- IS TOUCH A LEGITIMATE STAGE INPUT AT ALL? Genuinely unresolved in the sources. MusicRadar calls the QC 'the first touch-screen modeller that nailed editing using a touchscreen'; MusicRadar's OWN QC mini review says the touchscreen 'isn't the most responsive… also a common criticism levelled at the Quad Cortex'; gigging players on jazzguitar.be say touchscreens are 'too slow, too much looking required with missed touches' and that apps 'can be unstable, and wouldn't trust them on-stage'. The doctrine's answer (lock the glass, make everything foot-reachable) sidesteps the disagreement rather than resolving it — but it means the app must be fully playable with the screen ignored, which is a bigger scope commitment than it sounds.
- SHOULD HOLDING A BOUND SWITCH JUMP THE PHONE'S EDIT SCREEN TO THAT BLOCK? Helix's capacitive touch-to-navigate is simultaneously its most-praised navigation shortcut AND its most-complained-about false-positive source (there is a global kill switch in Global Settings because the false-positive rate is non-trivial). Given that FirePolicy::Auto already double-fires held switches and HID mode auto-repeats, the answer is probably no — but it deserves an explicit decisions.md entry rather than silence.
- ALIASES OR COPIES? Line 6 Stadium made setlists into pointers and blindsided users ('If you rename or save changes to any preset, all presets with the same name — in every setlist — will also change'); Kemper copies the rig into the performance slot so library housekeeping cannot mutate the gig, and gets praised for it. Stacks currently references tone ids, and the browse screen is constantly re-downloading and re-favouriting models. Whichever we choose, it must be visible ('used in 3 stacks') and the active rig's model files must be pinned and verified before a gig, not on first stomp.
- DOES THE BURNT-ORANGE ACCENT SURVIVE THE STAGE TEST? 0xffff4d00 on 0xff08070f has never been contrast-checked against WCAG's 3:1 large-text floor, and it has never been rendered in greyscale to confirm it still reads as 'this is live'. Also unmeasured: whether the always-animating status orb becomes noise under dark-cockpit status-by-exception discipline.
- WILL ANDROID LET A LOCKED PHONE KEEP RESPONDING TO BLE MIDI FOR A FULL SET? Untested. This is the difference between 'phone clipped to a mic stand, screen off, foot-driven' and 'phone must stay awake, foregrounded, plugged in, with every other app closed'. Doze, audio focus, BLE connection parameters under screen-off, and battery on a 12-hour pedal versus a phone driving NAM inference all need a real-set trial — the community's standing advice for iPads on stage is 'exit all other apps before downbeat and carry a backup device'.
- PRESET-PER-SONG OR GENERIC RIGS? Genuinely split in the literature — a whole thread titled 'The preset per song trap. Yuck.' against cover-band players who copy one-preset-per-song into setlist order before each gig. Do not pick a side; but does the app ship a setlist primitive at all, and is a setlist an ordered list of rig references or the RAM-resident 'bank' that makes switching cheap? Those are two different features that NAM Live conflates deliberately ('gapless switching of presets in a bank').
